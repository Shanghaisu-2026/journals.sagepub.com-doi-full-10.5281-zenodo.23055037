A Practical Study of Construction and Re-imaging in the Context of Modernist Painting

Shang Haisu iD and Li Haohong iD ✉ View all authors and affiliations

Abstract

This study examines image construction and re-imaging in modernist painting using a mixed-method approach. While prior research has focused on theoretical and historical perspectives, empirical relationships between conceptual, perceptual, and contextual factors remain underexplored. This study develops and tests a conceptual model positioning construction and re-imaging as mediating mechanisms in artistic production. Qualitative data were obtained through visual analysis and expert interviews, while quantitative analysis employed PLS-SEM with 250 art and design participants. Results show that artistic concepts and perception significantly influence construction, whereas socio-cultural context affects re-imaging. Construction predicts visual form, and re-imaging predicts artistic innovation. Mediation analysis confirms their intermediary roles. The findings contribute to empirical aesthetics by integrating art theory with quantitative validation and offering a structured framework for analyzing artistic processes.
